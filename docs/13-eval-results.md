## Why 2018-08-25

It is the only date where **all four sources are simultaneously present** over this
region — INCOIS analysis steps at 2018-08-20 and 2018-08-30, Argo day-files throughout,
GLORYS12 reanalysis coverage, and glider `ru29` flying inside the box during its densest
weeks (12 Aug – 2 Sep 2018). Chosen by measurement. Move the date and the glider is lost.

### The model needs QC too, and the brief does not say so

**44.42 °C is not a sea temperature.** 24 cells on the 2018-07-20 analysis step, at 75 m
and 100 m depth in the eastern box, read **36–44 °C**. At that depth in the Bay of Bengal
the water is 15–28 °C. A renderer that trusts its input paints a heatwave that never
happened.

What each defence actually catches:

| Defence | Result |
|---|---|
| **Argo global range test (QC test 6)**, limits −2.5 – 40.0 °C — published, not invented | **6 of 68,022 cells fail** (40.04 – 44.42 °C) |
| The product's own `TERR` error field | The bad cells sit at the **71st percentile** of TERR (min 7.5th, max 81.5th) — elevated, so they render fainter under L10, but **not a reliable detector** |
| Remaining gap | **18 cells between 35 °C and 40 °C pass both.** They are impossible *at depth* but legal at the surface somewhere on Earth |

Closing that gap needs a **depth-aware** range test. We do not have a published source
for depth-dependent limits, and inventing our own is exactly what `10-unsourced.md`
exists to prevent. It is logged there, unsourced, rather than guessed.

This is worth reporting to INCOIS.

## Model–observation co-location

| Source | N | Mean bias | Mean RMSE | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Argo** | 29 profiles | **−0.20 °C** | **0.71 °C** | Consistency check |
| **Glider** | 98 casts | **−0.69 °C** | **1.58 °C** | **Independent validation** |

Pooled over all levels. The depth-banded breakdown below is the number that should
actually be quoted — the pooled pair is not comparing like with like.

### The two numbers mean different things, and the difference is the point

`incois_argo_10d_VAM` is an objective analysis **of Argo profiles**. The 29 Argo profiles
compared against it are from inside its own analysis window, so most were almost
certainly assimilated into the very field they are being compared with.

> **0.71 °C is a consistency check, not a validation. It is a lower bound on true error.**

**The glider is not an Argo float and is not in that input stream**, so its 1.58 °C is a
genuinely independent estimate. The ordering is exactly what theory predicts —
independent error is more than twice the fit residual — and that coherence is itself
evidence the pipeline is sound. Had the glider come out *lower* than the Argo figure,
something would be wrong.

### Depth-banded, and this is the real result

Argo runs to 2000 m; the glider stops near 960 m. So a single pooled number compares the
glider *in the hard part of the column* against Argo *mostly in the easy part*. Banding
the residuals separates sampling from skill:

| Band | Argo n | bias | RMSE | Glider n | bias | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **0–300 m** | 3,583 | −0.37 | **1.26** | 3,407 | **−2.25** | **2.89** |
| 300–950 m | 2,984 | −0.19 | 0.35 | 7,651 | −0.02 | **0.31** |
| 950–2000 m | 3,768 | +0.03 | 0.19 | 237 | +0.20 | 0.23 |
| 50–950 m (matched) | 5,818 | −0.34 | 0.99 | 10,907 | −0.71 | 1.62 |

Three things fall out, and none of them are visible in the pooled figure:

1. **Below 300 m the two instruments are indistinguishable** — 0.35 against 0.31, with the
   glider marginally *better*. The analysis is genuinely good in the deep ocean, and an
   independent instrument confirms it.
2. **The entire disagreement lives in the upper 300 m**, where the glider shows a
   **−2.25 °C bias and 2.89 °C RMSE** against Argo's −0.37 / 1.26. That is the mixed layer
   and the thermocline — the part a cyclone forecaster actually cares about.
3. **Depth-matched, the gap survives but shrinks**: 0.99 against 1.62 over 50–950 m,
   rather than the 0.71 / 1.58 the pooled numbers suggest. So part of the raw gap was
   sampling, and part is real. Both parts are now on the record.

> **The operational reading:** this 1° analysis can be trusted below the thermocline and
> should be treated with caution above it, and the assimilated floats understate the error
> in exactly the layer that matters most. Surfacing that is what a 3D platform is *for* —
> it is invisible in a single pooled statistic and obvious the moment the column is drawn.

**What both numbers prove about correctness:** an inverted depth axis or a transposed
lat/lon dimension produces residuals of order 10 °C, not 0.7 or 1.6. Pressure-to-depth
conversion, the `sqrt(depth)` regrid, CF normalisation, the QC mask and the interpolation
all have to be simultaneously right to land here. This is what the fixture tests in
`server/tests/test_colocate.py` exist to protect.

## Tropical cyclone heat potential

The co-located pairs integrated into TCHP (`server/ocean/heat.py`): heat above the 26 °C
isotherm, the fuel a cyclone draws on, in kJ/cm². Same levels on both sides of each pair.
Harness output, verbatim:

```
Tropical cyclone heat potential  (analysis minus observed, kJ/cm^2)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  argo     29/33  casts reach D26   observed mean  51.97   analysis - observed  -3.48   rmse 13.16  (25.3 %)
  glider   95/112 casts reach D26   observed mean   9.46   analysis - observed +15.99   rmse 22.57  (238.6 %)
```

- **Against the assimilated floats the analysis is close on average** and scattered per
  cast. Consistency, not validation, for the same reason as the temperature numbers.
- **Along the independent glider track the analysis holds more heat than was measured**,
  in the same direction as the −2.25 °C upper-300 m glider bias above. The relative figure
  is large because the observed mean is small (cool water off Sri Lanka in the monsoon).
- **One deployment.** All 112 glider casts are `ru29`: one track,
  one fortnight. This is a finding about that water, not about the analysis everywhere.
- Constants: ρ 1025.0 kg/m³, cp 3991.868 J/(kg K)
  (TEOS-10 cp0) — both in `10-unsourced.md`. Casts that never cool to 26 °C on their
  accepted levels have no D26 and are excluded, not guessed.

## Assumptions carried on every record

Emitted with each comparison, because the source file does not state them:

- `units = "degs"` is not UDUNITS; read as `degree_Celsius`
- no `standard_name`; assumed `sea_water_temperature` from variable identity
- `ZAX` has no `positive` attribute; assumed positive-down because values are
  non-negative and ascending
- display window is ±5 days; the analysis's own assimilation window is wider
  (`T_BOXOBS` 28–30 per step against ~20 for a strict 10-day bucket)
